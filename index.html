MIDNIGHTCITY

A place doesn't sleep — it just changes what it looks like awake. Scroll through one night, hour by hour, as the light moves from window to window.

9:00 PM

Dusk

The sky burns out slow, orange folding into violet. One by one, office windows go dark and kitchen windows go gold. Somewhere below, a kettle starts, a bus sighs at a corner, the day changes shift with the night.

12:00 AM

Midnight

Rain finds the neon and multiplies it — every sign doubled in the wet street. The city is loudest here, all at once: a late train, a closing shutter, someone's laugh carried two blocks by the wind.

3:00 AM

Deep Night

This is the hour with no one in it. Streets empty enough to hear your own steps. Even the neon seems to dim out of respect. Just the sky, doing the one thing it does without an audience.

6:00 AM

Dawn

Windows go dark not because the city is done, but because it doesn't need the light anymore. The horizon takes over the job. Somewhere, someone is already awake, watching the gold come back on its own.
